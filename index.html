Principal, S.P.I.T.

I have great pleasure in expressing my thoughts as the Principal of Sardar Patel Institute of Technology (SPIT), the Numero Uno, self-financed, autonomous Institution of Maharashtra. We are a constituent of Bharatiya Vidya Bhavan, not just a conglomeration of more than 300 institutions, but a culture, a saga, a holy journey, started by Dr. K.M. Munshi with the support of Mahatma Gandhi in 1938. Imparting value-based education with Indian cultural ethos has always been the motto of Bhavan.

Engineers & technologists form the backbone of any nation’s economic development. The world is presently undergoing a very unprecedented, extraordinary, challenging time. A new normal is anticipated in many walks of life, including education. Such disruptions will come again and again in one or the other form. Engineers will work for 40–50 years of their life, they will have 3 to 4 diversified careers in technologies we are even unaware of. To make aspiring minds confident and future-proof, education must prepare them for a “marathon” rather than a “sprint”. SPIT makes continuous, sincere efforts towards this.

We focus on “How to learn?” rather than “What to learn?”. We believe in multidisciplinary exposure to the learners, yet ensuring growth in one vertical, cherishing human sensitivity and empathy. We have thoughtfully articulated a unique academic model towards this. Our splendid academic performance, sparkling placements (quantitative and qualitative), enrollment for higher studies at the best places of the world, prizes won by our students in national/international level technical competitions, in the past many years are the true testimonials for this.

With the support of a dedicated and hardworking faculty and staff, the institute has achieved enviable visibility and ranking in a short span. On behalf of all stakeholders of SPIT, I welcome you to this family and look forward to your valuable association with us for a better tomorrow. Four years of engineering education at Sardar Patel Institute of Technology or two years post-graduation, will undoubtedly empower you to lead a successful life.

“Let’s grow together…”

Sections 03 & 04
Vision, Mission & Program Outcomes
Our Vision
"To impart the education that graduates globally competent, socially sensitive, contributory computer professionals."

Our Mission

  1. To provide a rigorous, conducive academic environment and constantly innovate in the design of the curriculum and its deployment through excellence in Teaching–Learning–Evaluation–Assessment processes.
  2. To collaborate with industry and research organisations for knowledge creation and dissemination, fostering a culture of applied research and innovation among faculty and students.
  3. To inculcate professional ethics, social responsibility, and entrepreneurial thinking, preparing students to contribute meaningfully to society and the nation.

Program Educational Objectives (PEOs)

  • PEO 1: Graduates will have a successful career in the IT industry, academia, research, or entrepreneurial ventures, demonstrating core competencies in Computer Engineering.
  • PEO 2: Graduates will pursue lifelong learning and professional development, adapting to evolving technologies and contributing to multidisciplinary problem-solving.
  • PEO 3: Graduates will exhibit professionalism, ethical values, effective communication, leadership, and social responsibility in their professional and personal endeavours.

Program Outcomes (POs)

  • PO 1 Engineering Knowledge: Apply knowledge of mathematics, science, engineering fundamentals, and specialisation to solve complex engineering problems.
  • PO 2 Problem Analysis: Identify, formulate, review research literature, and analyse complex engineering problems using first principles.
  • PO 3 Design & Development: Design solutions for complex engineering problems that meet specified needs with consideration for public health, safety, and societal concerns.
  • PO 4 Investigation: Use research-based knowledge to investigate complex problems, design experiments, analyse data, and synthesise information.
  • PO 5 Modern Tool Usage: Create, select, and apply appropriate techniques, resources, and modern engineering tools for complex activities.
  • PO 6 Engineer & Society: Apply reasoning informed by contextual knowledge to assess societal, health, safety, legal, and cultural issues.
  • PO 7 Environment & Sustainability: Understand the impact of professional engineering solutions in societal and environmental contexts.
  • PO 8 Ethics: Apply ethical principles and commit to professional ethics, responsibilities, and engineering practice norms.
  • PO 9 Individual & Teamwork: Function effectively as an individual, member, or leader in diverse teams and multidisciplinary settings.
  • PO 10 Communication: Communicate effectively on complex activities, write reports, make presentations, and give/receive clear instructions.
  • PO 11 Project Management: Demonstrate knowledge of management principles and apply these to manage projects in multidisciplinary environments.
  • PO 12 Life-long Learning: Recognise the need for and engage in independent and life-long learning in the broadest context of technological change.

About the Department Curriculum & Syllabus Highlights

Redefining Engineering Education: The New-Age Curriculum

From Learning Technology to Creating Impact

Engineering education is undergoing a fundamental transformation. The engineer of today is expected to do much more than master technical concepts. Along with strong domain knowledge, students need the ability to think critically, communicate effectively, work across disciplines, understand emerging technologies, and contribute responsibly to society and industry.

Recognising this changing landscape, Sardar Patel Institute of Technology has introduced its Liberal "Pi-Model" of Engineering Education under the NEP 2020 curriculum framework. The curriculum is designed not merely to produce technically competent engineers, but to nurture well-rounded professionals with interdisciplinary awareness, ethical grounding, and the skills to adapt to a rapidly changing world.

The 160-credit structure distributes learning across Basic & Engineering Sciences, Programme Core, Programme Electives, Experiential Learning, Open Electives, and Skill Enhancement Courses — providing 57 different pathways to graduation.

A Curriculum Built Around Choice

The curriculum follows a Choice Based Credit System (CBCS) in which students shape their academic journey according to their interests. Programme-related courses account for 55% of the curriculum, while substantial credit space is allocated to multidisciplinary and experiential components. This flexibility allows students to gradually develop an academic profile aligned with their individual aspirations.

From Core Computing to Emerging Technologies

The initial semesters build strong foundations in Engineering Calculus, Linear Algebra, Probability, Digital Systems, Programming, and Data Structures. By the third and fourth semesters, students encounter core subjects — Discrete Structures, Database Management, Computer Organisation, Operating Systems, Software Engineering, and Design & Analysis of Algorithms. The fifth semester takes students into Artificial Intelligence & Machine Learning, Cryptography & Network Security, Theory of Computation, and Distributed Computing.

Experiential Learning: From Concepts to Creation

The curriculum places strong emphasis on experiential learning with a structured progression:

Learn → Explore → Build → Experiment → Solve → Create

Students undertake Mini Project I in Semester V, Mini Project II in Semester VI, followed by a Main Project, and depending on the selected pathway, research or industry internships. The curriculum introduces project-based learning much earlier than the final year, encouraging students to identify problems, experiment with solutions, and develop working systems.

Honours by Research

For students with strong research inclinations, the curriculum provides a dedicated Honours by Research pathway. Students earning 18 additional credits through prescribed research stages, with a CGPA of 8.25 or above, may pursue the Honours by Research degree — creating sustained research experience at the undergraduate level.

Multiple Exit Pathways

Aligned with NEP 2020, the curriculum provides exit pathways after one, two, and three years, leading respectively to a Certificate in Engineering, Diploma in Engineering, and Bachelor of Science degree, subject to prescribed credit and requirements. This ensures that every student's learning is formally recognised regardless of the duration of study.

Advisory Board & Board of Studies (BoS)

The Department maintains an active Advisory Board comprising eminent academicians from premier institutions and industry leaders. The Board of Studies (BoS) meets regularly to review and update curriculum, ensuring alignment with industry needs and emerging technologies. The Departmental Academic Performance Committee (DAPC) monitors academic progress, analyses results, and implements corrective measures.

Key Governance Bodies:

  • Board of Studies (BoS): Chaired by HOD with industry experts, alumni, and academicians as members. Responsible for curriculum design, revision, and approval of new courses.
  • DAPC: Departmental Academic Performance Committee — reviews result analysis, syllabus coverage (ISE 20%, MSE 20%, ESE 100%), and ensures Gaussian distribution in assessment.
  • NBA Compliance: Continuous monitoring of Course Outcome (CO) attainment, PO/PSO attainment yearly, and PEO attainment every 4 years.

Section 18
Departmental Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities & Challenges

The following SWOC analysis provides a candid assessment of the department's current position and future trajectory.

✦ Strengths

  • 98.21% placement rate with top-tier companies (Barclays, JP Morgan, BlackRock, Morgan Stanley)
  • NBA Accredited 4 consecutive cycles (2013, 2016, 2021, 2024)
  • Strong research output — 40 publications (15 journals + 25 conferences), 9 Scopus-indexed, 3 SCIE
  • 12 faculty with Ph.D., 6 pursuing — strong academic qualifications
  • NEP 2020-aligned Pi-Model curriculum with 57 pathways
  • Active student communities — CSI, IEEE, FACE, ACSES
  • 24 students placed at IIT Bombay for higher studies
  • Flagship SPIT Hackathon — 3,316 registrations nationally

✦ Weaknesses

  • Limited industry-sponsored research grants and consultancy revenue
  • Some faculty positions remain contractual/ad-hoc
  • Need for more dedicated research labs for emerging areas (Quantum, Robotics)
  • Limited international collaborations and exchange programmes
  • Doctoral programme could benefit from more enrolled scholars
  • Alumni database and engagement system needs digitisation

✦ Opportunities

  • Growing demand for AI/ML, Cybersecurity, and Cloud Computing professionals
  • NEP 2020 flexibility enables innovative programme design
  • Mumbai's thriving startup ecosystem — potential for incubation partnerships
  • Government initiatives like Digital India, Make in India create research funding avenues
  • Expansion of MDM partnerships with more industry leaders
  • International conference hosting to boost visibility
  • Patent portfolio growth through student-faculty collaborative research

✦ Challenges

  • Rapid pace of technological change demands continuous curriculum updates
  • Retaining top faculty in a competitive academic market
  • Balancing research output with heavy teaching loads
  • Increasing infrastructure costs for modern computing labs
  • Competition from new-age coding academies and online platforms
  • Ensuring consistent quality across all four divisions
